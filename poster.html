1. PROBLEM STATEMENT & DATA SCIENCE WORKFLOW SEC 2.1

Clinical Problem Context

Breast cancer is one of the most prevalent and life-threatening malignancies globally. Early detection drastically elevates survival rates (>98% 5-year localized survival). Manual examination of Fine Needle Aspirate (FNA) biopsies is tedious, subjective, and prone to diagnostic fatigue.

Research Question: Can Logistic Regression classify breast tumor malignancy with high precision and explainable feature weights from cell nucleus morphometrics?

4. FEATURE ENGINEERING & SELECTION SEC 2.7 - 2.8

Multicollinearity Removal (VIF Analysis)

Features were categorized into 3 groups (Mean, SE, Worst). Bivariate Pearson correlation revealed severe multicollinearity between geometric metrics: radius_mean ↔ perimeter_mean (r > 0.98) and radius_mean ↔ area_mean (r > 0.98).

Fig 5: Diagnostic Feature Correlation Heatmap Matrix

Selection: Dropped perimeter_mean and area_mean. Selected 7 independent predictors: radius_mean, texture_mean, smoothness_mean, compactness_mean, concavity_mean, concave points_mean, and symmetry_mean.

5. MACHINE LEARNING MODEL DEVELOPMENT SEC 2.9

Tri-Faceted Modeling Suite (CSE 4114)

  • 1. Supervised Classification (Primary): Logistic Regression with Sigmoid link function: P(Y=1|X) = 1 / (1 + e^-z). Outputs probability score and clinically transparent beta weights.
  • 2. Supervised Benchmarks: Decision Tree (max_depth=5) & Random Forest (100 estimators) trained on stratified 80/20 train/test splits.
  • 3. Unsupervised Clustering: K-Means (K=2) evaluated with Elbow Curve & Silhouette score (~0.41) demonstrating distinct patient groups without labels.
  • 4. Predictive Regression: Multiple Linear Regression predicting continuous tumor perimeter from radius, area, and texture.

7. RESULTS, DISCUSSION & CONCLUSION SEC 2.11

Key Findings & Clinical Takeaways

  • 1. Biomarker Dominance: Cellular contour indentations (concave points_mean) and nuclear enlargement (radius_mean) are the top physical drivers of malignancy.
  • 2. Clinical Interpretability: While Random Forest achieves 99.12% accuracy, Logistic Regression is preferred in clinical validation pipelines because its linear weights allow oncologists to explain every diagnostic decision.
  • 3. Linear Regression Accuracy: Perimeter prediction achieved R² = 0.9962 and MAE = 0.81 mm, validating tight geometric consistency across FNA biopsies.
  • 4. Project Limitations: Synthetic duplication was used to satisfy the 1,000-row lab quota. Mild class imbalance exists (62.7% vs 37.3%). External hospital cross-validation is needed before clinical deployment.
  • 5. Future Scope: Implement SMOTE resampling, evaluate non-linear Support Vector Machines (SVM), and deploy CNN computer vision on raw microscopy tiles.
